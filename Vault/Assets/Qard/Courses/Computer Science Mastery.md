The course has one starting point: the **Intro** of Fundamentals. Each group starts with an **Intro** objective for its hub note, and every other objective in the group opens only after it.

Inside a group the route is a tree: each objective has one prerequisite in the group, the topic it builds on most directly. Branches fan out from that topic instead of forming one long line.

- **Fundamentals**: Big O leads to the memory hierarchy; endianness leads to bit manipulation.
- **Data Structures** follows Fundamentals. Arrays open dynamic arrays, linked lists, spans and the hash-based structures. Linked lists open queues and trees. Hash maps open sets, composite structures and graphs. Trees open search trees, tries, range trees and the heap family.
- **Algorithms** follows the Data Structures Intro. **Paradigms** comes first, then **Patterns**; the individual paradigms and patterns branch off those two. The concrete families open from Patterns: Search Algorithms, Sorting Algorithms, Graph Algorithms and String Matching. Inside a family a variant follows the algorithm it refines, such as Heap Sort after Selection Sort or A-Star after Dijkstra.

An objective links to another group only where it is built on one structure, such as the heap for Heap Sort, Top-K and Dijkstra. Qard's Route here follows the prerequisites for a selected objective.

The existing objective identities, learning goals, linked sources, and assessment history remain the course record. Source-note lifecycle does not establish assessed mastery. Lessons and checks update objective states, due dates, and evidence.
